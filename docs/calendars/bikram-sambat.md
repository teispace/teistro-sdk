# Bikram Sambat: the source memo

Status: `draft`, opened 2026-09-05 with what the baseline engine's own
generator established; the research standard of
`09-guidelines/04-adding-a-calendar.md` (R1 to R5) is not yet met, and
the calendar ships as `Tabular` inside the published span and `Computed`
outside it until it is. The maintainer's mandate (2026-09-05): the SDK
must compute Bikram Sambat from first principles the way the Nepali
panchanga does, for any year asked (1700 BS included), with the
published spans as the authority inside their range, so that Nepal's
panchanga makers can use the SDK.

## What the calendar is

A sidereal solar calendar: a month begins when the Sun enters the next
sidereal sign (a sankranti), reckoned for Nepal, so months are 29 to 32
days and a year 365 or 366. Year Y begins at the Mesha sankranti falling
in AD year Y − 57 (1 Baisakh, mid-April). The month names are Baisakh,
Jestha, Asar, Shrawan, Bhadra, Ashwin, Kartik, Mangsir, Poush, Magh,
Falgun, Chaitra.

## R1: the authority

The government's Panchanga Nirnayak Samiti (the calendar-determining
committee) publishes the official month lengths; the baseline engine
carries its table for BS 1970 to 2095 as published and marks 2096 to
2100 as projected. The committee's own publications and its stated
method are not yet in hand: **open**.

## R2: the authority's publications

Not yet obtained; the table in the SDK is the baseline engine's copy
(rank 2). **Open.**

## R3: three independent real-world sources

Not yet cross-validated; the baseline engine validated its table against
published almanacs by a checker script, which counts as one. **Open.**

## R4: every day in range validated

Done for what exists: the SDK's calendar round-trips every day of BS 1856
to 2457, year totals are 365 or 366 throughout, and the anchors 1 Baisakh
1970 (13 April 1913), 2072 (14 April 2015) and 2081 (13 April 2024) hold.

## R5: the divergence envelope

What the baseline engine's generator measured, and the SDK adopts as the
starting point of its own engine:

- **Model.** Surya Siddhanta sidereal solar longitudes reproduce the
  official table far better than modern (drik) ones: 87.0 % of month
  splits and 61 of 126 year totals exact against BS 1970 to 2095, versus
  72.0 % for drik with the best cutoff. The official calendar is computed
  from the classical text.
- **Place and clock.** Kathmandu (27.7172 N, 85.324 E), with Nepal's
  offset history: local mean time (+5:41:16) before 1920, +5:30 until
  1986-01-01, +5:45 after. A sankranti near local midnight lands on the
  wrong civil day under the wrong offset and mislabels two months.
- **The day rule.** A fitted cutoff: a sankranti at or after 0.705 of the
  local civil day (about 16:55) makes the next day the first of the
  month; before it, the same day. Fitted, not quoted: the regional
  traditions differ (Orissa same day, Tamil before sunset, Malayalam
  before aparahna, Bengali before midnight) and none reproduced the table
  as well.
- **Continuity.** Year totals differ from the official ones in adjacent
  pairs (a day moving across a New Year and back), so the running day
  count stays within ±1 day across the whole span and never drifts; a
  computed year is reliable, a computed day near a month edge is not.
- **The residual 13 %** of month splits is the committee's own
  conventions and is exactly what R1 to R3 must uncover.

## The SDK's engine (planned; `docs/03-design/calendar-bikram-sambat.md`)

1. Sankranti instants from two sources behind one interface: the
   Surya Siddhanta solar model (the `siddhanta` crate of the module
   catalogue, with and without bija) and modern positions through the
   ephemeris port with the profile's ayanamsha.
2. The month-start rule as named variants, each a row: `SANKRANTI_DAY`,
   `THRESHOLD { fraction }` (0.705 as the fitted default), `SUNRISE_TO_SUNRISE`,
   `BEFORE_SUNSET`, `BEFORE_APARAHNA`, `BEFORE_MIDNIGHT`; the place and
   the offset history from the time layer, never hard-coded.
3. A measurement harness (`cargo xtask calendars bs-fit`) that runs every
   model and rule over the official span and reports month-split and
   year-total agreement, cumulative drift, and the divergence set; the
   default is whichever reproduces the table best, and the numbers are
   published here.
4. The shipped table is generated by that harness for any requested span
   (1700 BS or earlier included) and checked in with the frame stamped;
   a context holding an ephemeris can also compute a year that is not in
   the table, stamped `Computed`.
5. Inside the official span the table wins and a disagreement is reported
   as `Divergent`, never hidden.
