# Aayadi: a building's measure read by remainders

Status: `built`, 2026-11-03. Written from the sources before any code, for
the application's SR-35 (addendum 3): "Aayadi: aaya, vyaya, the nakshatra
and the others, each from its source".

## 1. What the reckoning is

Every vastu text that sizes a building tests its measure before the
measure is accepted. A dimension (a side, the sum of two sides, the
perimeter or the area) is multiplied by a small number and divided by
the size of a named series; the **remainder** names a member of that
series: the twelve aayas, the ten vyayas, the eight yonis led by the
banner (*dhvaja*), the 27 nakshatras, the seven weekdays, the 30 tithis
and others. A measure whose members are the good ones is built; one
whose are not is lengthened or shortened by a unit and tried again.

Nothing here reads the sky, so the reckoning is exact integer
arithmetic: the record takes a length and a breadth as whole numbers in
whatever unit the caller measures in (hasta, kol, daṇḍa or aṅgula) and
answers every quantity its source names, the remainder, the member it
names and each clause the source judges. The texts choose the unit by
the building, not the arithmetic: Samarangana Sutradhara XXVI vv. 13–16
takes towns in daṇḍas and houses in hastas, and if no good aaya falls in
hastas, the same measure in aṅgulas, pādas or yavas; Mahidhara's gloss
of *Muhurta Chintamani* XII vv. 11–12 says the same of aṅgulas. A caller
who wants a finer unit passes the measure in it.

Each system is read from a public-domain print, on the page images:

| `MANASARA` | *Mānasāra* | P. K. Acharya's text as his *Dictionary of Hindu Architecture* (1927) quotes it under *ṣaḍ-varga* | IX vv. 63–77, with XXX vv. 169–190, LII vv. 357–360 and LXIV vv. 69–82 |
| `SAMARANGANA` | *Samarāṅgaṇa Sūtradhāra* of Bhoja | Ganapati Sastri, Gaekwad's Oriental Series 25 (1924) | XXVI (*Āyādinirṇaya*) vv. 12–56 |
| `CHINTAMANI` | *Muhūrta Cintāmaṇi* of Daivajna Rama | Mahidhara's Hindi gloss, Lakshmi Venkateshwar Press, Kalyan (1892); the *Pīyūṣadhārā* (1945 print) | Vāstu-prakaraṇa XII vv. 3–12 |

*Vishvakarma Prakasha* II vv. 49–52 and 66–68 word the *Chintamani*'s

gives no remainder reckoning, so it supplies nothing here. Dagens's
*Mayamata* translation is in copyright and was not read.

## 3. The four systems

Notation: L the length, B the breadth, S = L + B, P = 2(L + B), A = L × B.
A row "× m ÷ d" is the remainder of the measure times m divided by d.

### 3.1 *Mayamata* IX vv. 20–24

| Quantity | Reckoning | Verse |
|---|---|---|
| aaya | S × 8 ÷ 12 | v. 20 ("vasu-nidhi-guṇite dineśa-dharma-hṛte") |
| vyaya | S × 9 ÷ 10 | v. 20 |
| yoni | S × 3 ÷ 8, the eight from dhvaja | v. 21 ("rāma-ghne 'ṣṭāpahṛc-cheṣam") |
| nakshatra | S × 8 ÷ 27, from Ashvini; the quotient is the building's age (*vayas*) | v. 22 |
| tithi | P ÷ 30 | v. 23 |
| vara | P ÷ 7, from Sunday | v. 23 ("sūrya-mukham") |

v. 20's measure is "vyāsāyāma-samūha", the sum of the breadth and the
length; *Mānasāra* IX lists the sum and the perimeter as different
measures, so the sum is read (C1190). v. 23 divides the perimeter with
no multiplier, and the print queries its divisor for the weekday (C1191).
The good yonis are the banner, the lion, the bull and the elephant
(v. 22); an aaya above the vyaya gives happiness and a vyaya above the
aaya ruin (v. 24). v. 19 asks that the measure not conflict with the
owner's and the site's stars, which the verse does not reckon (unjudged).

### 3.2 *Mānasāra* IX vv. 63–77

| Quantity | Reckoning | Measure (the "some say" split) |
|---|---|---|
| aaya | × 8 ÷ 12 | length |
| nakshatra | × 8 ÷ 27 | length |
| vyaya | × 9 ÷ 10 | breadth |
| yoni | × 3 ÷ 8 | breadth |
| vara | × 9 ÷ 7 | perimeter |
| tithi | × 9 ÷ 30 | perimeter |

IX allows the test on the sum, the length, the breadth, the perimeter or
the pada, and adds that "some" take the aaya and the nakshatra on the
length, the vyaya and the yoni on the breadth and the tithi and the
vara on the perimeter. The split is the default (Acharya's table follows
it) and a caller names one measure for all six instead (C1194). The
clauses: an aaya above the vyaya brings every prosperity, one below it
every fault (IX vv. 75–77); the weekdays of Venus, Jupiter, the Moon and
Mercury are good (IX vv. 78–81); the banner, the lion, the bull and the
elephant are the good yonis (LII vv. 359–360, LXIV v. 73). IX vv. 78–81
judge the nakshatra against the owner's in words that need their pada
and round; that stays unjudged.

### 3.3 *Samarāṅgaṇa Sūtradhāra* XXVI

| Quantity | Reckoning | Verse |
|---|---|---|
| aaya | A ÷ 8, the eight from dhvaja, laid from the east clockwise | vv. 17–18 |
| nakshatra | A × 8 ÷ 27 | vv. 35–36 ("tri-ghanena bhajet") |
| vyaya | the nakshatra's count ÷ 8 | v. 36 ("nakṣatre 'ṣṭa-hṛte vyayaḥ") |

The vyaya is one of three by its size beside the aaya (v. 37): equal,
*piśāca*; greater, *rākṣasa*; less, *yakṣa* — the new kind `vyaya_kind`.
The house is good with a lesser vyaya (v. 48) and is not built with an
equal, a greater or no vyaya (v. 51). The aaya's fruits are v. 21–22's:
the banner wealth, smoke distress, the lion enjoyment, the dog quarrel,
the bull wealth and grain, the ass harm to women, the elephant blessings
and the crow death; the bull, lion and elephant are praised for temples,
towns and houses (v. 20) and the banner everywhere (v. 23). With the
owner's star: the tara counted from it to the house's star, the 3rd, 5th
and 7th avoided and the 1st, 2nd and 8th middling (vv. 40–43), and the
house's gana the owner's (vv. 44–48, the catalogue's own gana).

The amsha (Indra, Yama, Raja: the vyaya, the area and the syllables of
the house's name ÷ 3, vv. 37–39), the house's name against the owner's
(v. 52), the signs of the two stars (vv. 53–55), the life of v. 56
(C1197) and the count of six good *karaṇas* (vv. 49–50) need a name or a
reading the record is not given, and are listed unjudged (C1196, C1198).

### 3.4 *Muhūrta Cintāmaṇi* XII vv. 4–12

| Quantity | Reckoning | Verse |
|---|---|---|
| aaya | A × 9 ÷ 8, the eight from dhvaja | v. 11 (v. 4: A ÷ 8) |
| vara | A × 9 ÷ 7, from Sunday | v. 11 |
| amsha | A × 6 ÷ 9 | v. 11 |
| dhana (wealth) | A × 8 ÷ 12 | v. 11 |
| rina (debt) | A × 3 ÷ 8 | v. 11 |
| nakshatra | A × 8 ÷ 27, from Ashvini | v. 11 |
| tithi | A × 8 ÷ 15, a tithi of either half | v. 11 |
| yoga | A × 4 ÷ 27, from Vishkambha | v. 11 |
| ayu (age) | A × 8 ÷ 120 | v. 11 ("kha-bhānu") |
| vyaya | the nakshatra's count ÷ 8 | v. 7 ("bhaṃ nāga-taṣṭaṃ vyayaḥ") |

v. 11 gives the multipliers as 9, 9, 6, 8, 3, 8, 8, 4, 8 and the divisors
as 8, 7, 9, 12, 8, 27, 15, 27, 120 (*kha-bhānu*, the digits 0 and 12
read from the units up). The 1892 table beside it agrees but for the
age, printed 102; Mahidhara's prose under it writes the weekday's
multiplier as 8 and the age's divisor as 12. Only the verse agrees with
itself in every cell, and it is built (C1193). v. 4's aaya (A ÷ 8) and v. 11's (A × 9 ÷ 8) never part,
as 9 leaves 1 over 8. v. 3 runs the reckoning backwards — the area for a
chosen star s and aaya a is (152 (s − 1) + 81 (a − 1) + 17) mod 216, a
larger house adding 216s — and so **checks the table against itself**:
152 leaves 0 over 8 and 152 × 8 leaves 1 over 27, 81 the reverse, 17
leaves 1 over both, so v. 3 lands on v. 4's aaya and v. 11's nakshatra for
all 216 pairs, and on a zero remainder for the 8th aaya and the 27th
star (C1192). The gloss's example is the acceptance value: Anuradha's
owner, Rohini chosen, the lion: 203, which as 29 by 7 hastas reckons the
lion, Rohini and vyaya 4.

The clauses: an odd aaya is good and an even one grief (*Pīyūṣadhārā* on
v. 4, quoting Vasishtha and Shripati; *Vishvakarma Prakasha* II v. 52);
the house's star the owner's own brings death (v. 12). Mahidhara's gloss
adds: a benefic's weekday good, wealth above debt good, the 3rd, 5th and
7th tara bad, a rikta tithi and the new moon bad, "vyatipata and the
other" yogas bad and the signs matched as in marriage. The ones it
names exactly are built; the new moon (a 15th that is either the full or
the new moon), the yogas (the set is not listed) and the signs are
unjudged. The amsha's meaning is not given (v. 7's Indra, Yama and Raja
are a different reckoning on the house's name, C1196).

## 4. The zero remainder

A remainder of zero names the series' last member: the 12th aaya, the
8th yoni (the crow), the 27th star (Revati), the 30th tithi (the new
moon), the 7th day (Saturday). *Muhurta Chintamani* v. 3 proves it for
its own series, since its own inverse puts the crow and Revati on zero.
*Mānasāra* IX v. 75 calls an aaya that divides wholly "full", so an aaya
and a vyaya compare by their counts, the zero being the full number.
*Samarangana* v. 51 forbids a house with no vyaya: there the vyaya's
zero is kept as none, the one place it is not the last member (C1192).

## 5. What the record answers

`almanac.aayadi` (`ts_aayadi`, the muhurta family) takes
`{system, length, breadth, owner?, measure?}`: the system key, the two
sides (1 to 1 000 000 each, whole units), the owner's star for the
clauses that read it, and for `MANASARA` only the measure. It answers
each quantity (the measure, its value, the multiplier, the divisor,
the quotient, the remainder, the count 1 to the divisor, and the
member it names), the vyaya's kind and the tara where they are
reckoned, every clause as `holds` with its verse, and the asks the
record does not judge. It never answers one verdict: a reader weighs
the clauses (`report-clauses-not-a-verdict`).

The new catalogue kinds are `vastu_yoni` (160: the eight from the
banner, the aaya of *Samarangana* and *Chintamani* and the yoni of
*Mayamata* and *Mānasāra*) and `vyaya_kind` (161). No printed example of
a whole reckoning was found in the public-domain prints read for
*Mayamata*, *Mānasāra* or *Samarangana*; their tests hold each verse's
arithmetic and the *Chintamani* gloss's example. Cruxes C1190–C1199.
