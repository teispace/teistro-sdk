**The roadmap's "BPHS ch. 2 vv. 67–78" is the Subodhini prints' numbering**,
and it fits exactly: v. 66 closes the shadow planets, the heading before
v. 67 reads *atha janmakāla iṣṭalagna-niścayārthaṃ gulikasādhanam āha*
("now, to settle the birth lagna, the reckoning of Gulika"), and v. 78 closes
the chapter.

**The prints disagree on the text, not only the numbers.** Jha's print
carries the Gulika verses (its ch. 3 vv. 66–70) but a *different* pranapada
passage: v. 71 defines the pranapada from the Sun's movable trine at one sign
for each quarter-of-a-sign's worth of pranas (15 palas), and vv. 72–74 judge
the birth **auspicious or not** by the pranapada's house from the lagna. Jha
then reproduces the Subodhini verses in a note as the reading of "a printed
book" and rejects them as interpolated, saying the trine-from-lagna reading of
birth-time correction is a later invention. Santhanam's translation (owned in
print by the maintainer, not read here) is believed to follow Jha's ch. 3
numbering; that needs checking against the print. By its OCR, Santhanam's
ch. 3 ends at the pranapada's calculation (vv. 71–74) and does not carry vv.
75–78; that too waits on the print.

**vv. 75–77, read again on the page images** (2026-10-08, for X6 and X7):
the 1899 print on leaf n40 (p. 12) and on a second scan of it
(`brihatparasharahorahiniditikagiridharalalsarmaparasurmabhattaed.sridhara_202003_201_s`,
leaf n33, whose OCR's *ahīnabale* is an OCR slip; the image has the
reph, *hīnabale*), the 1923 print on leaf n42 (p. 13), and Jha's note on
leaf n43 (p. 32). They agree but for v. 75's particle, *tadaiva* in 1899 and
*tadeva* in 1923. No public-domain print glosses vv. 75–77.

**The nearest parallel is Phaladeepika III.16** (the sanskritdocuments
e-text and Sastri's 1950 translation, pp. 31–32): Gulika's sign lord,
navamsha lord and navamsha sign with their trines, then *tadvad vidhau
balayute*, likewise from the Moon when it is strong, strength undefined.
Sastri's preface says he corrected this verse's metre; the 1937 first
edition's page would show the uncorrected text.

**The prints disagree inside the example.** The 1899 print reads 6;17 × 4 as
**25;2** (24 signs, and 17 palas over 15 = 1 sign and 2 palas left), then
10;2, then doubled 10;4: the verse's arithmetic. The 1923 print reads
**25;8**, 10;8 and 10;16, which is 6;17 multiplied as a time, and still prints
3s 4°. The 1899 figures are the consistent ones.

**The printed answer does not follow from the verse as written.** Two
readings reproduce 3s 4°: the remainder mod 12 (1s 4°) added to the Sun's
*sign* (Gemini) with no modality shift; or the printed "mod 15" remainder (10)
counted inclusively from Libra. The verse's own sequence (mod 12, from the
Sun, then the dual sign's 5th) gives Scorpio 4°. This is crux X3.

The table on p. 11 of the 1923 print gives the same chart's Gulika 5s 6° and
pranapada 3s 4° beside the shadow planets. No date or place is printed, so
the figure cannot be recast.

### IV.21 (conception to birth)

Sanskrit (1912, p. 89): *tatkālam indusahito dvirasāṃśako yas
tattulyarāśisahite purataḥ śaśāṅke / yāvān udeti dinarātrisamānabhāgas
tāvad gate dinaniśoḥ pravadanti janma*.

- Paraphrase: the number of the dvadashamsha (*dvirasāṃśaka*) the Moon holds
  at conception or at a query; when the Moon reaches the sign that many signs
  on, birth comes; birth is by day or night as the rising sign is a day or a
  night sign; and as much of the rising sign as has risen, so much of the day
  or night will have passed at birth.
- The verb is **pravadanti**, "they say": Varahamihira reports others (the
  1912 gloss names the author of Saravali); it is not his own rule.
- **Prints disagree on where to count from:** 1912 main text, from the sign
  the Moon holds (favoured by Gargi); Iyer (the commentator), from the sign
  after the sign the dvadashamsha is named for; a third view in the 1912 note,
  from Aries. Some authors take the rising **navamsha** for the rising sign
  (1912 notes 3–4; Iyer reads navamsha in the text itself). The 1912 gloss adds
  "in the tenth month" (some say the ninth).
- Iyer's worked example: the Moon in the 8th dvadashamsha of Aquarius (named
  for Virgo), counting 8 from Libra gives Taurus; the commentator refines to
  Rohini from the Moon's progress inside the dvadashamsha.

IV.22: a Saturn or Moon navamsha rising with that graha in the 7th delays
delivery to three or twelve years; and which of the chapter's yogas also hold
at birth is judged by their nature.

## 4. Methods not located on a public-domain page

- **Kunda** (the lagna's longitude × 81 should fall in the Moon's nakshatra
  or its trines): attributed in modern manuals to Prasna Marga and Kerala
  horas. Prasna Marga part 1 (`prasnamarga-035823mbp-1`) has unusable OCR;
  the verse was not found. Not shipped until it is (X13).
- **The baseline engine's tattva cycle** (unequal 6, 12, 18, 24, 30 minutes on
  a 90-minute cycle, started by the weekday lord's tattva, alternating
  direction; sex: earth, fire, ether male, water and air female) cites a 1970s
  exposition, not a text. It contradicts v. 193's durations and v. 294's
  mapping.
- **Event fitting against dasha boundaries** (the baseline engine's main
  stage): no classical source; a modern practice.
