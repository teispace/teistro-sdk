# KP, measured

Status: `generated` by `cargo xtask kp`. Do not edit: `check-kp`

The design this measures is `kp.md`. The corpus records no KP reading,
and the Readers' worked examples are the crate's own tests; what no
example says is **how far a reading can be trusted**, and KP stakes
everything on the sub lord. All 55 recorded births are founded under
`kp-default` (Krishnamurti's ayanamsha, Placidus, the true node), with a
polar day reckoned from civil midnight since Tromsø has no June sunrise
to start one.

## 1. How long a sub lord holds

A point's sub lord changes when the point crosses an edge of its sub.
Each margin is the arc to the nearer edge over the point's own rate,
taken from the chart founded half a minute either side of the birth, so
it reads as **the birth-time error that changes the lord**, whichever
way the time errs.

| sub lord of | median, minutes | shortest, minutes | under 1 min | under 2 min | under 5 min |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| the lagna | 1.86 | 0.17 | 17 of 55 | 32 of 55 | 55 of 55 |
| any cusp | 1.38 | 0.00 | 245 of 660 | 432 of 660 | 658 of 660 |

One birth in 2 would read its lagna's sub lord differently if its time
were two minutes out. The lagna's **sub-sub** lord holds a median of 10
seconds, which no recorded time settles. The Moon's sub lord holds a
median of 0.8 hours.

## 3. Horary numbers a place cannot raise

A number's chart puts its start on the ascendant (C156), which a
place can do only where that ecliptic point rises: everywhere under
66.55° of latitude, and not every point beyond it. Each
birth's place is asked for all 249 numbers at its own moment.

| birth | latitude | numbers refused |
|---|---:|---:|
| c028-troms-1988-06-21 | 69.65° | 81 of 249 |
| c029-troms-1988-12-21 | 69.65° | 81 of 249 |

Two of the charts' cusps came from a stand-in for Placidus, which the
profile's polar policy puts in where Placidus has no answer
(c028-troms-1988-06-21, c029-troms-1988-12-21).
