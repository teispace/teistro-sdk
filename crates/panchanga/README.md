The almanac of one day at one place.

| module | what it settles |

## Four things the corpus decided

A daily panchanga is almost entirely convention, and a convention guessed
wrong is a number that looks right. So every rule here was measured
against the 55 recorded days before it was written.

1. **The day is a window, and the window is `time`'s own `LocalDay`.**
   Sunrise to the next sunrise, on every recorded day. `day.day_boundary`
   — declared in Phase 1 and unread until now — moves it, and moves
   nothing else: a choghadiya divides the daylight whatever the window is.
2. **A limb is a span with both pairs of bounds.** The corpus records only
   the clipped view, so "the tithi began yesterday at 21:05" is a fact it
   cannot answer and this crate can.
3. **Every period is one division of one of two arcs**, so there is one
   divider — `core::interval::Interval::divided` — and the rest is tables.
   A period whose arc does not exist is **absent**, not an interval of no
   length: on a polar day the lists are shorter.
4. **The choghadiya is the hora's weekday walk.** The lord of the *k*th
   eighth of the daylight is the lord of the *k*th hora, over 1320 horas
   and 880 choghadiya without exception, so the whole of it is a
   seven-row table over a function `crates/time` already has.

## Three knobs, each a measured difference

| knob | why |
|---|---|
| `panchanga.centre` | an almanac is geocentric wherever the chart is; the lunar parallax reaches a degree, which is two hours of tithi boundary |
| `panchanga.moon_events` | the recording engine takes the Moon's rise and set over the civil day inside a sunrise-bounded section (registry entry 18) |
| `panchanga.muhurta_tables` | the yoga tables are the one limb the corpus cannot settle, so which tradition's rows are used is a setting |

## Against the corpus

`tests/baseline.rs` compares everything the corpus can decide without an
ephemeris, computed from the arcs the corpus itself recorded: 159
inauspicious eighths, 848 choghadiya with their lords, 1272 horas, 53
Abhijit muhurtas, 55 lunar months, 9 panchakas, the ayana and the disha
shool — every one to within a fortieth of a millisecond, which is the
last bit of a double near two and a half million. The classification is
compared against the Sun and Moon the corpus recorded at all 55 birth
instants.

Brahma muhurta is asserted **different** by the amount the registry
records — a median 10.0 seconds and at worst 27.6 — because the engine
sizes it from the night that follows the day and the SDK from the night
that ends at sunrise (entry 17). A test that avoided the subject would be
worth nothing.

What is not there is the limb *instants*: a tithi's boundary is a
position over time, and no provider inside this workspace has real
positions. `tests/kernel.rs` holds the kernel to its own properties over
the analytic test provider — the spans cover the window, are in order,
share their boundaries and carry both pairs of bounds; a tithi is two
karanas; a day inside a week is the same day — and the instants land with
the conformance harness over an adapter.
