# `teistro-aspect`

Status: `built`, 2026-09-07. The design is
[`docs/03-design/aspect-and-drishti.md`](../../docs/03-design/aspect-and-drishti.md);
the measurement that tested it before a line of this crate existed is
[`docs/03-design/aspect-drishti-measured.md`](../../docs/03-design/aspect-drishti-measured.md),
generated by `cargo xtask aspect` and held by `check-aspect`.

Which bodies reach which, and how strongly.

| module | what it settles |
|---|---|
| [`drishti`](src/drishti.rs) | the graha drishti: one table, the three special grahas, and the node's houses as a settings choice |
| [`rashi`](src/rashi.rs) | the Jaimini rashi drishti, a relation between signs that always looks back |
| [`conjunction`](src/conjunction.rs) | co-presence in a sign, which is what the tradition means by "with" |
| [`orb`](src/orb.rs) | the shared engine: a separation against a stated tolerance, applying or separating |
| [`chart`](src/chart.rs) | the assembly: every relation of a founded chart |

No port, no ephemeris, no instant — everything is arithmetic over a
founded chart.

## The corpus records no aspect

This is the first Phase 4 module the corpus cannot check. The pass
established it by searching **every key of all 115 fixture files** for a
name an aspect could have been recorded under, and the fixture schema
carries none either. So the rules rest on the project's own research
page, and the tests carry the weight the corpus usually would:

1. **Invariants over the whole space, not samples.** Twelve signs,
   twelve houses, twelve grahas: 1728 relations and 11 664 ordered pairs,
   exhausted.
2. **Agreement with the falsification pass**, which computes the same
   tables and never imports this crate. Its published counts — 1020
   mutual pairs, 48 beyond the seventh, 3679 relations over the corpus —
   are asserted here, so two independent derivations have to keep
   agreeing.
3. **The corpus's own placements** for those counts. It cannot say
   whether an aspect is right, but it can say how many there are.

## Three things the measurement settled

- **A mutual full aspect is the seventh — or Mars and Saturn.** The pass
  proposed the first alone and the measurement **refused** it: 48 of the
  1020 sign pairs are not the seventh. Saturn three signs after Mars
  stands in Mars's fourth and holds Mars in its own tenth, and both are
  full. It happens on 7 of the 93 recorded charts. (The other case is
  Jupiter with itself across a trine, which is arithmetic rather than
  astrology — which is why `mutual` takes a pair of *bodies*.)
- **A drishti is counted from the sign.** Over this corpus that is
  indistinguishable from counting by house, because all 837 recorded
  placements are whole-sign whatever chalit a fixture names. They part
  on any chart whose houses come from cusps.
- **The two systems are different relations.** Over 6696 ordered pairs
  they agree on 4053, and each sees relations the other does not.

## What does not ship

**The sphuta drishti.** The degree-based value Drik Bala weighs has no
source in this project: the research page records that a construction
exists and never writes it down, and the corpus records no drishti value
to fit one to. Registered as crux C45; `aspect.drishti_table` refuses
any key but the one it has, by name. `Strength::virupas` gives the
*whole-sign* value (a quarter is fifteen), which is the specification any
future construction has to meet at each house — what is missing is the
interpolation between them.

**A node's aspect beyond the seventh** stays the root's `NONE`. The knob
offers three readings, the corpus prefers none, and `NONE` claims least.
The other two make a node a special graha with a chosen pair of houses,
exactly as Mars, Jupiter and Saturn have fixed ones.

## A drishti is a step function

A whole-sign relation changes all at once at a sign edge, so it carries
the **distance** to that edge from both ends and ships no threshold —
the same argument `state::boundary` makes, and `Boundaries` now lives in
`teistro-core` because both want it. `Drishti::is_firm` takes the
tolerance as an argument, because it is a property of the caller's
provider and not of the tradition.

## What this gave `state`

The pass retried the three lajjitadi `crates/state` had refused, with a
real drishti. None of the tradition's rules is exact — and **none misses
a single recorded reading**, while adding the aspect clause makes each
one worse. So the condition is necessary, the engine applies something
narrower, and it is not a drishti. `state` now answers `Holds::No` with
certainty where the condition fails: 1301 of 1953 open questions closed,
with no aspect model needed to do it.

## Tests

54: 26 beside the rules they test, 9 exhausting the space, 4 over the
corpus, 10 end to end over a founded chart, and 5 doc examples.

- `tests/exhaustive.rs`: every graha against every house, every pair of
  signs under both systems, every ordered pair of grahas and signs for
  mutuality, and every node reading against every body.
- `tests/corpus.rs`: the pass's published counts, recomputed through
  this crate.
- `tests/chart.rs`: the assembled relations, mutuality listed once and
  symmetric, and — the `state` lesson as a test — **every shipped
  profile names a drishti table that resolves**.
