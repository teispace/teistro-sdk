## What the corpus decided

Every input is recorded beside every answer, so a formula reproduces a
recorded value or it does not — the `vargas` shape, not the `aspect`
one. **Six of the eight rules are exact**, to the last bit of a double,
over all 71 fixtures that carry them. The project's own research page
marks the whole family "verify"; the pass is that verification.

Four things the measurement settled that a reading of the sources would
not have:

1. **The five the Sun casts are a chain**, and two of its steps are
   reflections — so Dhuma, Indrachapa and Upaketu advance with the Sun
   while Vyatipata and Parivesha retreat. Written as five offsets, two
   would carry the wrong sign and look right. `solar::advances` states
   each direction and a test measures it.
2. **The Sree lagna is a fraction of a circle**, not of a sign. The
   three other readings are wrong by tens of degrees.
3. **The time-driven lagnas start at the Sun *at birth***, not at
   sunrise as most statements of the rule say.
4. **Gulika begins Saturn's eighth and Mandi ends it** — start against
   end, not start against middle, and two readings of one portion rather
   than two names for one point. Derived by trying all twenty-four
   candidate instants against every recorded value, not proposed.

## The clock they read is not the ishtakaal beside them

Each of the three clock-driven lagnas is exact on 46 of 71 fixtures and
out on the rest by an amount **proportional to its own rate** — 0.82° at
30° an hour, 1.63° at 60°, 2.04° at 75°.

That proportionality is the finding. Three rules wrong in proportion to
their rates are three *right* rules reading one wrong clock, and the
pass confirms it: on every fixture the three imply the same elapsed time
as each other to under a hundredth of a minute, and against that time
all three are exact. The difference from the recorded ishtakaal is at
most 1.633 minutes — registry entry 25, not a rule.

## What does not ship

**The Varnada.** Every recorded value is a whole sign, which is a fact
about the field worth having; the received rule is not — it is wrong on
31 of 71, and so is every variant the pass could build from the same
parts. That is crux **C22** met in the data: five published schools
disagree and this engine follows one of them. The catalogue keeps the
`VARNADA_LAGNA` key for whoever cites a school. A guess here is a wrong
*sign* in a chart, silently.

The other 38 of the catalogue's 49 points — Bhava and Vighati lagna,
Indu lagna, Kala, Mrityu, the sphutas, the arudhas — have rows and no
formula. The corpus records none of them, so each waits on a citation.

## No provider, and no new knob

Everything is arithmetic over a founded chart. The two day-division
points need the **ascendant at an instant that is not the birth**, and
that is a sidereal time and a latitude rather than an ephemeris, so
[`Ascendant`](src/eighth.rs) is a trait the caller supplies: `chart`
implements it over `astro::houses`, and a test implements it in one
line.

And no settings knob. The one thing the tradition genuinely divides over
— where inside Saturn's eighth the point falls — the corpus settles, so
a knob for it would be one nothing could choose between on evidence.

## Tests

25: 20 beside the rules they test, 5 over the corpus, 7 end to end over
a founded chart.

- `tests/baseline.rs`: every recorded upagraha and special lagna through
  this crate, at the pass's own bounds — exact for the six that are
  exact, inside the published bracket for the three the clock drives,
  and the Varnada's absence asserted as a deliberate one.

  them, the two the day divides joining when an ascendant is given, and
  — as `aspect` does — every shipped profile computing.
