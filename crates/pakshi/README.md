# teistro-pakshi

Pancha Pakshi, the five birds of the Tamil tradition, read from Agastya's
*Pañcapaṭci Sāstiram* (the 1880 Kanchipuram print) and P. V. Jagadisa
Ayyar's tables in *South Indian Customs* (1925), both public domain
(`docs/03-design/pakshi.md`):

- the native's **bird** from the birth nakshatra, the dark half's reversal
  a choice;
- each bird's **activity** in every yama of a day or night, from the one
  rule that generates all four printed tables (each half's sequence, the
  first yama's eater by weekday, and its step);
- the **sub-periods** of a yama, their order and owners, and their lengths
  by Agastya's nazhigai or the modern per-half figures;
- the **death bird** of each weekday and half, the birds' **relations**
  by Agastya's directed lists or the modern neighbour cycles, and their
  natural strengths;
- a day's **yamas** as instants, stretched over the real day and night or
  fixed at six nazhigai from sunrise.

No ephemeris, no chart: a day is its sunrise, sunset, next sunrise,
weekday and paksha, which the façade reads from the almanac.
