The divisional charts: one table-driven evaluator, twenty-one rows.

| module | what it settles |
|---|---|
| [`scheme`](src/scheme.rs) | the model and the twenty-one rows: a classifier, one group per group of signs, and each group's spans and map |
| [`evaluate`](src/evaluate.rs) | the kernel: a longitude in, a placement out, by integer arithmetic |
| [`chart`](src/chart.rs) | a whole chart of a founded moment, the mixed axis, vargottama |
| [`change`](src/change.rs) | when a body leaves the sign a chart put it in |

## One evaluator

```text
sign = (a·rashi + step·part + offset) mod 12
```

with `a` nought (count from a fixed sign), one (from the sign itself) or
the chart's own number (count continuously round the zodiac). Eighteen of
the twenty-one step; three list their signs instead, and there is no
second family — only two ways to fill one table.

## What the corpus decided

A divisional chart is a function of one sidereal longitude, and the
corpus records the longitude and the answer, so this is not a comparison
within a tolerance: every placement is right or wrong.

**19 530 recorded placements over 93 fixtures and two zodiacs, all
right.** Four of the fixtures are *tropical*, which moves every longitude
twenty-four degrees and lands the bodies in different parts of different
signs — a rule fitted to one zodiac would not survive them.

Three things the measurement corrected in the design page:

1. **The spans belong to the group.** D30's odd signs are cut 5, 5, 8, 7,
   5 degrees and its even signs the same widths reversed, so one chart
   has two span rules. The page's schema had `spans` above both.
2. **`divisions` names the chart** and is not always its part count: D30
   is called thirty and cuts a sign into five.
3. **Vargottama is a property of a body, not of a graha.** The recording
   engine never marks a lagna vargottama, and on two recorded charts the
   lagna's navamsha sign *is* its rashi sign (registry entry 20).

## Exactness

The part index is integer arithmetic on the canonical angle (ADR-0016).
The recording engine computes it as `floor(deg / (30/N))` in floating
point, and none of 30/7, 30/11, 30/27 or 0.2 is representable, so a body
exactly on a part boundary can land either side of it depending on the
platform. `tests/exhaustive.rs` asserts the boundary of every part of
every chart, in nanoarcseconds.

## Tests

41 of them, and none samples where the space is enumerable.

- `tests/baseline.rs`: every recorded placement, the rashi chart against
  the engine's own sign index, the vargottama difference asserted as a
  difference, and the tropical fixtures separately.
- `tests/exhaustive.rs`: the design's eight invariants over all 16 728
  cells of the twenty-one shipped charts and nine arbitrary ones — the
  spans fill a sign, every cell names a sign, the rule agrees with its
  materialised table, D1 is the identity, a cyclic chart's cells cover
  the twelve signs evenly, and every part boundary is decided.
- `tests/change.rs`: the sign is constant between two changes, the
  changes join up, a finer chart changes more often, and the
  trimshamsha's changes land on its own five-degree boundaries.
- `tests/chart.rs`: the assembled chart is what placing each longitude
  alone gives, the mixed axis moves the lagna and not the grahas, and
  vargottama is refused for a chart that is not the navamsha.

## Arbitrary D-N

There is no classical rule for D37. Producing one takes a chosen
convention — `vargas.unattested_dn` names the cyclic (parivritti) one —
and the result carries the convention in its provenance, which is what
makes it reproducible rather than merely plausible. `Scheme::cyclic`
accepts 1 to 300 and refuses the rest by name.
