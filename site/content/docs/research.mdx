title: A study over many births
description: sdk.research — counts and permutation tests, every rule a predicate, the family corrected and the study registered by its hash.
---

`sdk.research` asks whether a rule holds more often in one group of
births than in another, or than chance allows, without letting a study
find what is not there. A study names its **births**, its **rules** (each
one a predicate, read once on every chart), its **labels** and its
**test**, and the answer comes back per rule, never as one verdict.

```js
const tested = ctx.research.compare({
  births,
  rules: { shipped: ['YOGAS'] },
  design: { groups: births.map((_, i) => (i < 16 ? 1 : 0)) },
  test: { seed: 2026, permutations: 999, contrast: { kind: 'CASE_VS_REST', case: 1 }, alpha: 0.05 },
});
console.log(`registered as ${tested.provenance.inputHash}`);
const best = tested.rows.reduce((kept, row) => (row.p.value < kept.p.value ? row : kept));
console.log(best.predicate, best.p.value, best.adjusted.maxT);
```

This is the shared example `research`, executed in every binding by
`cargo xtask check-parity`, which holds that Node, Python, Dart, Rust and
Java print the same lines. Its labels mean nothing, which is the case a
study's corrections exist for: run it and see what the smallest raw p
becomes once the family is accounted for.

## The four studies

| operation | the question | the null |
|---|---|---|
| `counts` | how often each rule holds in each group | none: the first table, with the unreadable and the unstable counted apart |
| `compare` | is a rule commoner among the cases than among the rest, or does its share differ between any of the groups | the labels permuted, inside strata when the design has them |
| `expected` | is a rule commoner in this sample than in its own population | the sample refounded with clock times shuffled among its births, date and place kept (Gauquelin's control) |
| `timed` | is a rule delivered by a dasha at the subjects' own events more often than chance | events shuffled among the subjects, by date or by age |

## What keeps a study honest

- **The study is registered before it is read.** The answer's
  `provenance.inputHash` seals the births, the rules, the labels and the
  test, and is the same in every binding. Publish it before the data are
  collected; anyone can rerun the study and get the same hash and the
  same rows.
- **Every rule is one family.** Each row carries its raw p and the
  family's max-T, Holm, Bonferroni, Benjamini–Hochberg and
  Benjamini–Yekutieli adjustments. Max-T uses the rules' real dependence
  from the same permutations.
- **The permutations are fixed in the request.** There is no adaptive
  mode, so a study cannot draw until it likes the answer, and the
  answer's `resolution`, `1/(m + 1)`, is the smallest p the request can
  say. An `alpha` the permutations cannot reach over the family is
  refused rather than answered.
- **A chart a rule cannot be read on is not counted absent.** A birth
  whose recorded time is uncertain is read at both edges, and a rule that
  differs between them is counted unstable and left out of the
  denominator.
- **A seed is a whole 64-bit number** in every binding: a `bigint` in
  Node, a `BigInt` in Dart and a `BigInteger` in Java past what a plain
  number holds.
