---
title: An almanac
description: A week's panchanga in one crossing — the five limbs as spans, the day's periods, and nothing absent printed as a sentinel.
---

A chart is consulted once; a panchanga every morning. `sdk.almanac`
computes the page a Nepali or Indian almanac prints, and a whole range of
days in **one crossing**: consecutive days share a boundary, so day n's
next sunrise is day n+1's sunrise, and seven days cost much less than
seven days asked for one at a time.

Each day carries its sunrise and sunset, its lunar month and paksha, the
five limbs, the periods it is planned around and the Moon's rising and
setting. The first day prints:

## A limb is a span, not a name

"Today's tithi" is a question with two answers on most days, and the SDK
gives both, with the instant each gives way, which is what an almanac row
prints. A span carries its **own** bounds as well as the clipped ones, so
"the tithi began yesterday at 21:05" is a fact you can print:

```js title="bindings/node/example/almanac.mjs"
const printed = spans
  .map((span) => {
    // `whole` is the member's own span and `inside` the clipped one,
    // so a member that began yesterday says so rather than looking
    // as though it began at sunrise.
    const began = span.whole.from < span.inside.from ? '‹' : ' ';
    const ends = span.whole.to > span.inside.to ? '›' : ' ';
    return `${began}${name(span.member)} until ${clock(span.inside.to)}${ends}`;
  })
  .join('  ');
```

The `‹` and `›` above are those bounds: every limb that morning began the
day before, and the last of each runs past the next sunrise.

## Absent is absent

A value a day may not have is absent, never a sentinel: a day with no
sankranti has `null`, not Julian day zero, and a Moon that does not rise
inside the window contributes no event at all.

```js title="bindings/node/example/almanac.mjs"
if (day.sankranti !== null) {
  console.log(`  ${'sankranti'.padEnd(10)} the Sun enters a new sign at ${clock(day.sankranti)}`);
}
```

## One day

A day on its own is the range of one, unwrapped: the same crossing, and
the answer is a day rather than a list of one. It also carries the day's
horas and muhurtas:

```js title="bindings/node/example/almanac.mjs"
const one = ctx.almanac.day({
  date: date(Calendar.Gregorian, 2024, 6, 21),
  place,
  utcOffsetSeconds: 20700,
});
```

```text output="almanac"
day(one)       शुक्रबार  24 horas, 30 muhurtas, 16 choghadiya
```

`bindings/node/example/panchanga.mjs` computes the five limbs by hand
from two longitudes, to show the arithmetic underneath; an application
should ask `sdk.almanac`, which finds when each limb ends.

## Nepal's official days

Nepal's patro is computed by the Nepali committee from the Surya
Siddhanta: the tithi, nakshatra and yoga by the text with the committee's
bija, the sunrise from a modern ephemeris. That setup is a **preset** in
every binding, the committee's profile over a chain whose first entry is
the text: the Surya Siddhanta takes the engine after it for its sunrise.
An app passes its engine, the Teimeris adapter's descriptor; with none,
the sunrise is the built-in's, which is how this runs anywhere.

The modern reckoning, `nepali-default`, reads the same day from the
modern sky, and the two name another sunrise tithi on a few days a month.
The reason is always a tithi's end against the sunrise, so each row
prints both ends:

```text output="nepal_official"
2025-11-01  sunrise 06:16  official Ekadashi until 02:55  modern Dashami until 09:27
2025-11-02  sunrise 06:16  official Dwadashi until 01:26  modern Ekadashi until 07:47
2025-11-09  sunrise 06:21  official Chaturthi until 10:02  modern Panchami until 02:11
2025-11-10  sunrise 06:22  official Panchami until 08:04  modern Shashthi until 00:24
2025-11-11  sunrise 06:23  official Shashthi until 06:24  modern Saptami until 23:24
5 of 30 days name another sunrise tithi
```

The first row is Haribodhini Ekadashi, which Nepal kept on 1 November
2025 (Kartik 15, 2082): by the text the Dashami had ended before sunrise,
and by the modern sky it ran until 09:27.
